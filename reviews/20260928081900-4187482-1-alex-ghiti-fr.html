Patch summary
This patch series aims to improve the performance of zswap shrinker on systems running iocost by addressing two issues: (1) zswap writeback is issued as root and charged to the cgroup afterwards, causing stalls in application allocations; and (2) throttled writes sleep in their submitter, often an application thread. The patches modify zswap to mark writebacks as REQ_BACKGROUND instead of REQ_SWAP, allowing them to be throttled by iocost controllers. Additionally, patch 4 moves reclaim-driven writeback to a per-lruvec kworker, reducing the impact on application threads. The series includes benchmarks showing significant performance improvements for various workloads.

Alexandre Ghiti 2026-09-30 Reviewed-by Needs Work LLM
Reviewer noted that the patch does not handle the case where a kworker is already running on the same lruvec, and requested a check to prevent concurrent writeback operations. The reviewer noted that the current implementation may not scale due to the lack of worker accumulation, and requested further testing and discussion. Reviewer noted that the throttled writeback bio will incur debt in the cgroup and suggested deferring it until the cgroup has sufficient budget, as direct reclaimers would stall on application latency and kswapd reclaim would stall on a single cgroup's IO budget. Reviewer noted that enabling the zswap shrinker results in a significant decrease (~97%) in the zswap pool size, which they believe acts as an additional throttling mechanism and may prevent 'hot' pages from being drained. Reviewer noted that the work piled up in the shrinker control loop when writeback couldn't catch up, but considered this to be a desired behavior. Reviewer noted that the current implementation could lead to starvation of other memory cgroups when one worker exhausts its memory budget, and suggested this as a reason for the performance issue.
