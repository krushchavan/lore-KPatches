Patch summary
This patch adds infrastructure for virtual swap devices, which have specific requirements and behaviors that differ from physical swap devices. Without this special casing, undesirable problems can arise, such as incorrect allocation of swap slots and per-CPU caching issues. The patch addresses these problems by adding a check to ensure that the correct device is being used for each operation, allowing for more efficient and reliable use of virtual swap devices.
Chris Li 2026-09-24 Inline Review Reviewed-by Needs Work LLM
Reviewer Chris Li expressed disagreement with making vswap a special device, citing the need for xswap to be treated as a normal swap device to avoid issues like lock ordering violations and per-CPU allocation caching problems. Reviewer Chris Li acknowledged the patch's design choice as a careful one, but noted that it usually comes with both pros and cons, indicating a neutral sentiment. Reviewer Chris Li questioned the patch's approach to handling uncompressible pages in xswap, suggesting that xswap could store them within itself instead of rejecting them, and noted a similarity with zswap's behavior. Reviewer Chris Li noted that xswap doesn't require a lower tier and should not need to check for swap_is_xswap(), as it lacks the baggage associated with zswap's writeback enable interface. Reviewer Chris Li noted that the issue of swap tiering and priority is not specific to vswap, but rather a problem area in the core swap layer, which will be addressed by a separate series. Reviewer Chris Li suggests using Baoquan's writeback series as a base for the patch, citing it as being closer to his intended design and a more suitable foundation for further development. Reviewer Chris Li expressed disappointment that the original author did not follow his suggestion to use a proper swap_ops approach, which would eliminate the need for most of the swap_is_vswap() checks. Reviewer Chris Li expressed concerns that the vswap patch will break existing /etc/fstab configurations, including support for UUIDs, and change user-visible behavior by enabling vswap without explicit user action. He suggested adopting a design philosophy similar to VFS-like file systems for swap, where each swap device has its own superblock. Reviewer Chris Li expressed strong disagreement with the patch series, citing that it is 'unclean' due to excessive special cases and requested a more general solution.
