[PATCH v2 0/3] mm: make watermark checks honour ALLOC_NOFRAGMENT

View on lore.kernel.org →
Submitted: 2026-11-05
Active on: 2026-11-05
Patch summary
This patch series addresses a performance issue in the Linux kernel's memory management, specifically when using defrag_mode. The high-order watermark check currently counts free pages of every migratetype, leading to incorrect allocation decisions and stalls in compaction. Patches 1-2 modify the watermark check to consider the request's migratetype, while patch 3 informs direct reclaim that the allocation is ALLOC_NOFRAGMENT.
Harry Yoo 2026-11-05 Reviewed-by Positive LLM
Identified the root cause of the issue as the high-order watermark check ignoring the allocation's migratetype, leading to premature stopping of direct compaction and reclamation. Suggested modifying the changelog for clarity.

On Mon, Nov 02, 2026 at 09:39:34AM +0000, Kiryl Shutsemau (Meta) wrote:
> The high-order check in __zone_watermark_ok() accepts a free page of any
> migratetype.  Under ALLOC_NOFRAGMENT, a request below pageblock_order can
> only take a page of its own migratetype or claim a whole free block, so
> the check can pass when the allocation cannot succeed.
> 
> Telling those apart needs the request's migratetype, but not every check
> wants them told apart.  A check whether an allocation can be served now
> has to count only the pages it can take.
>
> A check whether there is enough free memory counts every migratetype:
> kswapd and kcompactd balancing a zone, order-0 thresholds, the
> slowpath's forecast whether reclaim and compaction can still make
> progress.  Reclaim and compaction work across migratetypes to free whole
> pageblocks.
