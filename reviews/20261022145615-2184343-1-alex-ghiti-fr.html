Patch summary
This patch aims to reduce the number of I/O requests on a swap device by keeping reclaim batches contiguous. The current implementation of swap allocation and deallocation can lead to scattered writes, resulting in increased I/O requests. The patch modifies the allocator to allocate contiguous free slots for a reclaim batch, reducing the number of small requests and improving throughput. This is achieved by informing the allocator about the size of the batch and allowing it to allocate contiguous slots accordingly. The goal is to reduce the overhead of swap I/O operations and improve system performance.

Reviewer Nhat Pham expressed support for the patch, noting that there are still workloads that benefit more from disk swapping than compression swapping, and requested that the swap subsystem be made to work for them. The reviewer agrees with the patch's approach of allocating contiguous swap slots, noting that it makes sense to not apply the heuristic for large folios. Reviewer Nhat Pham noted that the patch's approach of preferring non-full clusters over free clusters may lead to increased lock contention and fragmentation in swap files, but believes it will be fine as long as contiguity is found for the first time in the batch. Reviewer Nhat Pham questioned the effectiveness of the patch's approach, noting that it may constantly overshoot the estimate of pages to be reclaimed due to rotating/promoting references, which could create problems down the line. He suggested collecting a batch of pages after unmapping and passing them to the phys swap allocator as an alternative solution.
