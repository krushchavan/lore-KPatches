Patch summary
This patch addresses the issue of high overhead in memcg stats flushing, particularly at high depths (8-16) where the extra latency can be up to 48ns. The problem arises from the use of rstat rate-limiting, which is arbitrary and bounded by time rather than actual stats updates. The proposed solution involves minimizing unnecessary overhead by not updating stats that don't require precision, and removing the rate-limiting mechanism. This approach aims to strike a balance between precision and performance, especially in scenarios where lock contention is a concern.

Reviewer Yosry Ahmed noted that the rstat rate-limiting is arbitrary, relying on a fixed two-second time interval rather than stats updates, and suggested removing it to allow callers to have a higher stats updates threshold based on their error tolerance. Reviewer Yosry Ahmed expressed concerns about introducing two separate update schemes, one for stats and another for other cases, which he believes would add unnecessary complexity. FWIW, when I added the per-memcg thresholding for flush (i.e.
stats_updates), there were some regressions in some microbenchmarks on
the update side due to the parent iteration in deep hierarchies.
However, we decided the regressions were acceptable because they were
of low significance compared to the extra of charging/uncharging in
those deep hierarchies. As you said, charges are not 1:1 to stat
updates, but it was considered a general indicator of acceptable
performance for the workload at that depth. That is all obviously very
hand wavy :)
