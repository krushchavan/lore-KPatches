Patch summary
This patch proposes to allow virtualized swap to be treated as a separate tier from storage-backed swap, similar to how memory tiering works in the system. The idea is to reduce overhead by allowing compressed memory to stay mapped while read-only, thus avoiding faults and improving performance. This approach is inspired by zsmalloc's existing tiering concept and aims to address the issue of 'ghost-pressure' caused by software-compressed data requiring faults. The patch also clarifies that virtualized swap should be considered a physical resource, not just a logical one, aligning with historical definitions and usage.
Kairui Song 2026-09-26 Inline Review Reviewed-by Needs Work LLM
Reviewer noted that the effort to push memsw into V2 upstreamly is ongoing, and suggested holding a topic on it at LSFMM last year but couldn't due to lack of time. Reviewer noted that minimizing virtualization overhead is crucial and emphasized the importance of careful design in achieving this goal. Reviewer noted that the proposed solution is similar to swap tiering and expressed uncertainty about its implementation, specifically worrying that adjusting the limits might lead to different compression rates. Reviewer noted that the virtualization issue is more compositionally complex and requires accounting for ordering, writeback mechanics, and migrations. Reviewer noted that the faulting process may be slowed down significantly by hardware dependencies and certain mitigations, which could impact performance in some cases. Reviewer noted that direct kernel monitoring is less effective and requested consideration for this aspect in the virtualized swap implementation. Reviewer noted that swap entries are similar because they're both page fault-based, which is a common point, but didn't provide further technical details or suggestions. Reviewer suggested giving ZRAM a lower priority in the swap hierarchy to reduce its impact on CPU usage for offline batch tasks, citing examples of unusual but valid use cases.
