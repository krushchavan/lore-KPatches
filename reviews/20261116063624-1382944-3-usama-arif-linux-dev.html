[PATCH v2 2/4] mm: don't PTE-batch a swap-in of a folio with a hardware-poisoned page

View on lore.kernel.org →
Submitted: 2026-11-16
Active on: 2026-11-16
Patch summary
This patch series addresses a bug in the Linux kernel's memory management, specifically when handling large folios (memory allocations) that contain hardware-poisoned pages. The problem arises from the fact that the PTE-batching path does not check for poisoned pages before mapping them. This can lead to unexpected behavior and potential crashes. The patch series proposes a fix by adding checks to ensure that each page in the folio takes its own fault, preventing the mapping of poisoned pages.

On Sun, Nov 15, 2026 at 10:34:56PM -0800, Usama Arif wrote:
> do_swap_page() checks PageHWPoison() on the faulting page, but the
> large-folio batching path then maps the rest of the folio without
> looking at its other pages. A poisoned page that is not the one being
> faulted gets mapped anyway, and the fault that would have reported it
> never happens.
> 
> Skip batching for such an anon folio and let each page take its own
> fault, where the existing check catches it. do_set_pmd() already backs
> off the same way.
> 
> Leave a freshly read folio alone: it is not anon yet and can only be
> mapped as a whole, and falling back would free it with the poisoned
> page still in it, which the retried fault can then get back from the
> page allocator.
